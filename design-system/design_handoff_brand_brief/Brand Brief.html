Make things that feel like Meadowbrook.

This page is for anyone making something with our name on it — a poster, a flyer, a social post, a sign on a gate. It explains who we are, what we sound like, and the colours and type to use so it all hangs together. Read it once. Keep it open while you work.

The story

Ten acres, bequeathed by the village, for the village.

Meadowbrook is the recreational heart of Dartington — a wooden 1960s community building, grassy mounds, an outdoor pool waiting to be restored, a playground, a bar, playing fields, a bike track and nature trails.

The land — ten acres on the edge of the village — was generously bequeathed by the Elmhirsts and the Dartington Hall Trust in 1962. It is managed by The Dartington Recreation Association (the DRA), a registered charity (1168817) run entirely by local volunteers, under a lease that next comes up for renewal in 2118. We have time.

Meadowbrook is what people know — the place, the building, the field at the end of the road. The DRA are the people who make it work. Both names matter; we use them differently. Meadowbrook goes on the gate, the signs and the social posts. The DRA signs the grant applications, the newsletters and the thank-yous.

A place to swim, kick around, eat pizza and stay too late.

What it actually is, on the ground

The site is a patchwork. A pool dug by hand by the village in the 60s, now closed and waiting for a restoration that's almost fully funded. A wooden community building that's seen better days but hosts a bar, a snooker room, a studio and a lounge. Playing fields across Colin's Bridge. A pump track and dirt jumps in the woods over the brook. A playground that gets battered and stays loved. Grassy mounds the community dug when they built the pool — kids still roll down them.

The chameleon principle

The brand is built like the site itself: a calm, papery core that steps back to let each facility have its own feel. We call these zones — the Pool, the Snooker Room, the Bike Track, the Playground, the Playing Fields, the Studio, the Lounge, the Extravaganza. Each one has its own paint colour, its own display typeface, sometimes its own motif. Underneath, the layout, the body type, the voice and the spacing all stay the same. So eight things can look like eight different things and still obviously be Meadowbrook.

Three things to remember

One — photography over illustration. The site does the work. Don't draw what you can photograph. The only big illustration we use is the hand-drawn site map, which is always shown whole.

Two — sentence case, never title case. "Book a table", not "Book A Table". The tone is a neighbour, not a corporation.

Three — no borders. If you need to separate two things, use a background shift or more space. Never a 1px line.

Voice

How we sound. Warm, direct, optimistic but grounded.

Sounds right
  • "Come as you are."
  • "The pool is closed for now. Help us bring it back."
  • "A place to swim, kick around, eat pizza and stay too late."
  • "Cross the bridge, find the fields."
  • "Bring chalk and patience."
Doesn't sound like us
  • "Experience World-Class Recreation At Dartington!"
  • "🏊 Splash Zone Active 🏊"
  • "Unlock your full potential with our state-of-the-art facilities."
  • "Premium Membership Tier Available Now"
  • "Synergistic community engagement opportunities."

Write like you're talking to a neighbour. "We" and "us" mean the whole community, not just DRA staff. Headlines are 2–4 words. Body copy gets to the point. No emoji in formal materials. Acknowledge the rough edges — a pool that needs restoring, a building that's seen better days — without apologising.

Marks

One hand, sun behind it.

The hand

A green hand, sun rays behind it. The Meadowbrook + DRA primary mark. Use the PNG from assets/logos/hand.png; don't redraw it.

Lockup

Hand mark, then "Meadowbrook" set in Plus Jakarta Sans Bold, with "The Dartington Recreation Association" as a small uppercase eyebrow beneath. Sentence case in body — proper noun only.

Don't

Don't recolour the hand. Don't add a stroke. Don't put it inside a circle. Don't trace it as an SVG. Don't drop it on a busy photograph — give it papery cream or solid green to sit on.

Warm highlights, slightly lifted shadows. Greens lush and true, never teal-shifted. Skin tones natural — never orange, never desaturated. Mix close-up texture (cladding, ripples, dough) with mid-range activity shots. Wide establishing shots are useful but shouldn't dominate.

House rules

The few things that will make work feel like Meadowbrook, or not.

Do

Sit content on papery cream #FBF0DF when in doubt. It's the safest, most "us" canvas.

Don't

Don't use bluish-purple gradients, multi-stop spectrums, or any kind of generated noise texture.

Do

Use real photography where you'd otherwise reach for an icon or illustration.

Don't

Don't add 1px borders, hairline dividers or stroke outlines. Use a background shift or more space.

Do

Pick one zone per piece. Use its colours, its display font, its motif if it has one.

Don't

Don't redraw the hand logo as SVG, change its colour, or place it inside a circle.

Do

Use sentence case everywhere except proper nouns. "Book a table", not "Book A Table".

Don't

Don't use emoji in formal materials. Informal social posts may use them sparingly.

Do

Always show the hand-drawn map whole. It's hand-drawn, slightly imperfect — keep it that way.

Don't

Don't crop, zoom or recolour the map. Don't generate AI imagery for any Meadowbrook use.
